# f116v, the last page

Notes from Koen Gheuens' video "The part of the Voynich Manuscript we CAN read (f116v). Why is it still so hard?" (Voynich Talk, Dec 2025) plus the usual references.

f116v has four lines written mostly in Latin script, in a hand datable to the first half of the 15th century, the same age as the parchment. So whoever wrote it was probably close to the making of the book. It's still barely understood.

What seems solid:

- **poxleber / pox leber**: *leber* = liver (Middle High German). The first part names the animal; "bock" (buck/goat) is the usual reading, with odd spelling. Compare *Rindesleber*, *Hüns leber*, *pokchsleber* in the CoReMA medieval German recipe corpus.
- **so nim**: "then take", the standard opener of a step in German recipes, normally followed by an ingredient.
- **gaismi(l)ch / gasmich**: probably *Gaismilch*, goat's milk. The missing *l* is unusual and not attested elsewhere.
- **Maria** with a cross: invocation of a holy name, common in charms.
- **anchiton / michiton**, **oladabas**: no convincing reading. Letter shapes are clear but the words don't mean anything known; at best forced semi-Latin.

So: German (dialect) recipe/charm vocabulary mixed with Latin-ish words and some Voynich glyphs, and a goat theme. Gheuens presented it to several specialists in inscriptions; all agree it's hard.

Why this matters for us: it's the only place where the scribe(s) may have written in a known language. Any proposal for the main text that can't say anything about f116v is missing the one crib we have. Possible experiments:

- check which Voynich-script glyphs appear inside the f116v lines and whether they follow the main text's slot rules
- compare letter forms on f116v with the Voynich glyphs that look like Latin letters (the "a", "o", "y" and "8"-shaped ones)

References in the video description: CoReMA (gams.uni-graz.at/o:corema), voynich.nu solvers page, multispectral images (Manuscript Road Trip, Sept 2024), Kwakkel (2014) "The Last Page of the Medieval Book", voynich.ninja thread 437.
