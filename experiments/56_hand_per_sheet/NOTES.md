# 56 — Is the sheet setting a writing session? The handwriting says: not visibly

## What I think

Exps. 40-42 found the way words are built is set per sheet. If that's just the state of a writer
at one sitting (pen, mood, speed), the handwriting should drift with it: glyphs drawn wider or
narrower, lines packed tighter, spaces wider. If it's a deliberate choice (a key, a setting), the
handwriting needn't follow.

## What I did

From the word boxes of exp. 55, four scale-free measures per page (every page was photographed at
its own scale, so everything is divided by the page's median box height): glyph width (box width per
EVA sign), line spacing, space width, words per line. Then exp. 42's comparison (same leaf, other leaf
of the same sheet, pages facing in the binding but on different sheets) and a Mantel test between
handwriting distance and fork-setting distance over all page pairs in a hand and section.

## Result

Handwriting distance relative to any two pages of the group:

| | same leaf | other leaf, same sheet | facing, other sheet |
|---|---|---|---|
| hand 1 herbal (A, 91 pages) | 0.77 | **0.71** | 0.85 |
| hand 2 herbal (B, 20) | 0.83 | 0.97 | 0.55 (2 pairs) |
| hand 2 bathing (B, 19) | 1.07 | 0.94 | 0.89 |
| hand 3 stars (B, 24) | 0.64 | 0.86 | 0.86 |

Handwriting vs fork setting (Mantel r): herbal A 0.10 (p 0.04), herbal B 0.09 (0.23), bathing 0.06
(0.32), stars 0.09 (0.25).

## So what

In hand 1's herbal pages the handwriting is a little more alike within a sheet than across facing
pages, and a little more alike where the fork setting is alike: weak traces of writing sessions. In
Currier B there's nothing: the bathing pages, whose fork settings are the most sheet-bound of all
(exp. 42), show no sheet structure in their handwriting at all.

So the setting is not just a writer's physical state that also shows in the pen. In B in particular
it changes from sheet to sheet while the hand stays the same. That points, weakly, toward a choice
made per sheet rather than a drift.

## Caveats

- The boxes were drawn by a transcriber (voynichese.com), not traced from the ink; box width and gap
  measure the tracer's boxes as much as the scribe's hand.
- Four coarse measures; real palaeographic features (stroke angle, ink density, glyph forms) need the
  images at full resolution, which I don't have here.
