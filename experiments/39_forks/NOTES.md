# 39 — Do the forks follow the scribe or the subject?

## What I think

Exps. 36-38 left a clean picture of *how* the words are made: one small table walked left to
right, with forks (start with *qo-*, *o-* or *ch-*; take a gallows or not; end in *-edy*,
*-aiin* or *-ol*). The open question is whether anything decides the forks. If the text says
something, the forks should follow the subject. If they're the writer's habit, they should
follow the hand and barely move with the subject.

Davis's five hands cross the illustrated sections in a few places, which lets the two be
pulled apart: hand 2 wrote herbal, balneological and text-only pages; hand 3 herbal and stars;
hand 1 herbal and pharmaceutical; herbal pages in Currier B were written by hands 2, 3 and 5.

## What I did

Each page with 60+ words becomes a profile of 34 fork choices (how its words start, which
gallows, how they end). For each comparison, R² (how much of the page-to-page variation the
grouping explains) against the same pages with their labels shuffled, and how often a page's
group can be guessed from the other pages (leave one out, nearest centroid).

Control for drift: within one section of one hand, the first half of its pages against the
second half (folio order). If the forks just drift as the writing goes on, two halves of the
same section should differ too.

Same hand, different subject: hand 2's herbal and bathing pages are told apart 95% of the time
from fork choices alone. On the bathing pages the same scribe starts a quarter of the words with
*qo-* (herbal: 9%) and ends a quarter in *-edy* (herbal: 14%), with more *-ol* and fewer *-ar*
and *-dy*. The subject explains more of the variation (R² 0.31) than the hand does within the
herbal pages (0.16), and as much as the Currier A/B divide does.

Within a section the halves are no more different than random halves (except a faint trend in
hand 1's 84 herbal pages). Whatever changes between sections doesn't creep along page by page.

## So what

The forks are not free and not just a scribe's fixed habit. The same writer, running the same
table, takes the forks differently on herbal pages and on bathing pages, and keeps it steady
for twenty pages at a time. That is what a text about different subjects would do. It is the
first result in this series that points toward the content mattering rather than away from it.

It isn't proof of meaning. Two other readings fit:

- **Sessions.** Hand 2's herbal pages sit in quires D-G and the bathing pages in quire M. If each
  section was written in one stretch with a different setting of the procedure, the halves test
  wouldn't see it (no drift inside a section), but the sections would still differ. Telling this
  apart needs pages of two subjects written in the same sitting, and the bifolium structure
  (exp. 29) is the only clock I have.
- **A deliberate setting per section.** A generator with a switch per section produces this too.

Either way, the next test is clear: *within* a section, do fork choices follow the picture? Herbal
pages show different plants; if the forks change from plant to plant beyond what neighbouring
pages share, that is content at page level, which neither sessions nor a per-section switch
explains.

## Caveats

- Small groups: hand 5 wrote 6 herbal pages, hand 3 six; the text-only comparison (4 against 1
  page) says nothing.
- The fork profile is coarse (first two signs, gallows, ending). It was fixed before looking at
  the results.
