# 63 — The rhythm as a two-sign boundary rule (didn't work)

## What I did

Exp. 62 showed the qo / ch-sh rhythm is words ending in *-y* (mostly *-dy*, *-ey*) being followed by
*qo-* words. The generator linked a word's start to the previous word's last sign only; here it looks at
the last two (`link_signs=2`), same protocol as exps. 60-61.

## Result

| | rhythm (ch/sh-y → qo, over base) | class sequence | within range (of 11) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Currier B, odd sheets | 1.96 | 0.063 | – |
| link on 1 sign + run copying (exp. 61) | 1.56 | 0.000 | 6 |
| link on 2 signs | 1.61 | 0.020 | 5 |

## So what

Looking two signs back barely moves the rhythm (1.56 → 1.61; B is 1.96) and costs elsewhere. The
rhythm isn't just a longer boundary rule: *qo-* after *-dy* depends on the family of the word that ends
in *-dy* too (after *ch-/sh-…-dy* 0.41, after *qo-…-dy* 0.38, after *o-…-dy* 0.35). That's the one
piece of word-to-word structure left that no generator of mine makes. It's small, it holds in every
section of B and at physically wide spaces, and it has no word order or grammar behind it (exps. 52-53).
