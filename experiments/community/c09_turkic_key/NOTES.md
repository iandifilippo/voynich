# c09 — The Turkic sound values (Ata Team, 2018)

## What was claimed

Ahmet Ardıç and his sons proposed in 2018 that the manuscript is Old Turkic written
phonetically. A voynich.ninja member listed the sound values from their video (o = o, y = u,
a = e, e = c, ch = ç, k = t or l, t = b, d = s, l = p or x, r = r, n = ng, iin = iin or m,
m = k, q = d, …) and pointed out two things that look wrong on their face: *m*, which lives
at line ends, is given k, and *q*, which only starts words, is given d.

## What I did

The forum objections are right and easy to put numbers on. Then the c07 test: read every
word that uses only these signs, try every allowed alternative, count how often one of them
is a word in Turkish, Azerbaijani, Turkmen or Uzbek. Compare with the same values dealt to
the signs at random, and with two texts that contain no Turkic: the copying model and a
Naibbe cipher of Pliny.

## Result

- 70% of words ending in *m* end a line; 99.4% of *q* begin a word. A sound like k or d has
  no reason to care where the line ends or the word begins.
- The key does beat its shuffles: 20% of words read as a Turkic word against 7% (p = 0.005).
- **So does everything else.** Copying model: 20% against 7%. Naibbe cipher of Latin: 17%
  against 6%. Per language the three texts are within a couple of points of each other.

## So what

The key isn't reading Turkic out of the manuscript; it reads Turkic-looking strings out of
anything with these glyph statistics. Same verdict as c07 and c08, for the same reason.
