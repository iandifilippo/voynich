# 58 — The zodiac labels: no calendar in them

## What I think

The zodiac pages have about thirty nymphs per sign, each labelled. Thirty per sign invites a
calendar reading: degrees or days, named. If the labels named the days, two signs would share names,
or at least label *i* of one sign would resemble label *i* of another. Neither needs the language.

## What I did

Ten signs (Aries and Taurus each split over two half-diagrams, joined here), 294 labels. Reuse across
signs, against the exp. 36 sign chain fitted on all labels of the manuscript and asked for the same
number of labels per sign (20 runs). Then, for the five signs with exactly 30 labels, agreement on
start, gallows, ending and length between labels at the same index (ZL's order around the rings) and
at different indices, with the order of the second sign also reversed.

## Result

| | share used in more than one sign | distinct / all |
|---|---|---|
| zodiac labels | 0.23 | 0.84 |
| label machine, 20 runs | 0.28 (highest 0.32) | 0.81 |

Same index against other indices: ratios 1.01-1.04 in the same order, 0.83-1.11 reversed: nothing.

## So what

The zodiac labels are reused across signs a little less than independent draws from the label machine
would be, and the label at a given place in one sign says nothing about the label at that place in
another. There's no set of thirty names and no count running round the rings, at least none that shows
in word shape. They behave like the other labels (exp. 45): words built fresh, mostly starting with
*o-*.

## Caveats

- ZL lists labels ring by ring; if transcribers started at different points on different signs, the
  same index isn't the same place. A rotation search would fix that but also make chance agreement
  easy to find; I didn't do it.
