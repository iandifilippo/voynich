# 42 — The setting belongs to the sheet

## What I think

Exp. 41's clusters inside a section were made of leaves and of conjoint leaves. If that holds
up, the setting was chosen per sheet: the folded piece of parchment that makes two leaves (four
pages) and, in the bound book, sits far apart (f103 and f116 are fourteen leaves apart).

## What I did

For every pair of pages in the same hand and section, the distance between their fork profiles,
relative to the average pair in that group (1.00), by how the two pages are physically related:
same leaf (recto-verso); same side of one sheet (a verso with b recto, a recto with b verso);
opposite sides of one sheet; and pages that face each other in the bound book but belong to
different sheets (verso of one folio, recto of the next). Sheets at the centre of a quire left
out (there conjoint leaves also face each other). Intervals by resampling sheets.

## Result

| | same leaf | same side of a sheet | opposite sides | facing in the binding, other sheet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hand 1 herbal (A) | 0.64 | 0.66 | 0.64 | 0.85 |
| hand 2 herbal (B) | 0.60 | 0.78 | 0.73 | 0.63 (2 pairs) |
| hand 2 bathing (B) | 0.62 | **0.52** | 0.81 | 0.88 |
| hand 3 stars (B) | 0.55 | 0.61 | 0.69 | 0.82 |

Same sheet against facing pages on another sheet: −0.20 (95% interval −0.36 to −0.05) in herbal A,
−0.36 (−0.58 to −0.13) in bathing, −0.21 (−0.32 to −0.12) in stars. Hand 2's herbal pages have
only two such pairs and say nothing.

Same side against opposite sides of a sheet: −0.29 (bathing, p ≈ 0.07), −0.08 (stars, p ≈ 0.10),
0.01 (herbal A). Suggestive in Currier B, absent in A.

## So what

Two pages on the same sheet share their setting even when they sit ten or fifteen leaves apart
in the book; two pages that face each other in the book but belong to different sheets share it
less. The setting was chosen per sheet, and the sheets were written as units before they were
put in their present order.

That fits exp. 29 (scribes 2 and 3 wrote sheet by sheet) and Davis's proposal that the book was
first a stack of single sheets (singulions), not quires, and it gives it a new kind of evidence:
not ink or layout, but the way the words on a sheet are made. In Currier B there's a hint that
the unit was one side of the unfolded sheet, which is what you'd expect if a sheet was written
flat, one side at a time.

What it says about meaning: the procedure had a state that was set once per sheet. That is what
a key changed per sheet would look like, or a generator re-seeded per sheet. Either way, the
sheet, not the page or the bound book, is the unit to analyse from here on.

## Caveats

- Small numbers of pairs per relation (8-39); the facing-pages control has 10-21 pairs.
- Sheet structure is taken from the quire/bifolium labels in the transcription file
  (Zandbergen's collation). Where the collation is uncertain, pairs may be mislabelled.
- Profiles are noisy for pages near 60 words.
