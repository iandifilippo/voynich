# 43 — A fixed-table cipher gives pages no identity

## What I think

If the manuscript is a cipher of the Naibbe kind (each letter or pair becomes a Voynich-like
word through fixed tables), its pages carry the plaintext's statistics and nothing else. Exp. 40
showed that pages of real texts are barely recognisable from word shapes. So a fixed-table
cipher should give pages no identity either, unlike the manuscript.

## Result

| | own half closest | shared words removed | chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Naibbe cipher of Pliny (fixed tables) | 0% | 0% | 2% |
| its plaintext | 1% | 1% | 2% |
| manuscript, four sections | 34-73% | 31-63% | 2-5% |

## So what

A verbose cipher with one set of tables for the whole book can't produce the page (sheet)
identity of exps. 40-42; the plaintext's own letter statistics don't move enough from page to
page. If the manuscript is a cipher, the tables or the key changed with every sheet. Together
with exp. 35 (no fixed small table of word halves) that leaves, among ciphers, only schemes with
a per-sheet key, which is a lot of machinery for a 15th-century cipher and still has to match
exps. 34-38.

The same goes for a generator: whatever made the text was re-set at each sheet.
